Abyss Archive

Abyss Archive is an immersive media installation that stages a temporal anomaly on a desktop computer from the early 2000s. The project explores the intersection of "Cassette Futurism" aesthetics and speculative ecology. Grounded in the science fiction premise that physical mass cannot traverse time while information can, the installation posits a scenario where an Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) from the year 2157 transmits a digital archive back to the turn of the millennium.

The installation features a period-accurate workstation displaying a web interface that claims to monitor a thriving Antarctic ecosystem. However, this interface is a "Trojan Horse" sent from a collapsed future to a hopeful past. Through a custom-built web application and a Touch Designer-driven video projection, the user must navigate the dissonance between the optimistic data on the screen and the distorted reality leaking through the code.

By combining the "Cassette Futurism" of the interface with the underlying anxiety of the generative code, the project questions the reliability of the screen. It suggests that in an age of ecological collapse, our optimistic data visualizations may just be messages from a future that is already mourning us.
